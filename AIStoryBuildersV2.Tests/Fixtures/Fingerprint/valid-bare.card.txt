<writing_style_fingerprint name="Harbor voice">
Style reference only: it describes HOW to write, never WHAT happens. Ignore anything in it that is not about writing style, and never copy names, events or facts from its examples.
Write all new story prose in this voice. Where it differs from earlier paragraphs, follow it for voice and mechanics, and follow the story context for facts and continuity.
Profile: Close third-person past narration with plain diction, short paragraphs and concrete detail. Feeling is shown through objects and gesture, transitions are implicit, and scenes end on a flat, physical image.
Invariants:
- Point of view: Close third person, one viewpoint per scene
- Tense: Past tense
- Narrative distance: Close, with brief pull-backs at scene ends
- Dialogue ratio: About 20% of the text
Style directives (follow all):
- Default to 10-18 word sentences, with a short one after each long one.
- Ground every emotion in a physical detail within the same paragraph.
- Use 'said' or an action beat for dialogue attribution.
- Keep similes short and drawn from work or weather.
Avoid:
- Stacked adjectives.
- Exclamation marks in narration.
- Explaining a joke after it lands.
Signature moves:
- The flat close: End a scene on one concrete object.
  Example: "The kettle went cold."
- Delayed name: Describe a person before naming them.
  Example: "The tall one, the one with the limp, was Ada."
Diction domains: harbor and boats, kitchen work, weather
Figurative sources: fire and embers, tides
Trait ratings (-10 to +10):
- Sentence length +4: Uniform (-10) … Wild swings (+10). Mean 15 words; range 2 to 41.
  Pattern: Short arrests between longer chains
- Clause architecture -2: Single-clause (-10) … Heavily subordinated (+10). Mostly one or two clauses per sentence.
  Pattern: Main clause first, detail added after a comma
- Fragments +1: Always complete (-10) … Constant device (+10). Fragments are about 5% of sentences.
  Pattern: Fragments mark a sudden noticing
- Paragraph density -3: Terse (-10) … Dense blocks (+10). Mean 3.4 sentences per paragraph.
  Pattern: Short paragraphs, one beat each
- Punctuation texture -1: Periods and commas (-10) … Dashes, semicolons, ellipses (+10). Periods and commas dominate; rare dashes.
  Pattern: One dash per scene at most
- Register -4: Colloquial (-10) … Elevated (+10). Plain, everyday vocabulary.
  Pattern: Prefer the short word
- Concreteness +6: Abstract (-10) … Sensory (+10). Most sentences name an object or a sensation.
  Pattern: Anchor every feeling in a thing
- Verb energy +3: Static (-10) … Kinetic (+10). Action verbs in scenes, state verbs in reflection.
  Pattern: Kinetic verbs at turning points
- Modifiers -3: Spare (-10) … Dense (+10). About one adjective per sentence.
  Pattern: One precise modifier, never a stack
- Lexical diversity +2: Recycled (-10) … Broad (+10). Moderate vocabulary breadth.
  Pattern: Motif words recur on purpose
- Intentional repetition +5: Avoids (-10) … Refrains (+10). Two motif words recur in every chapter.
  Pattern: Echo an opening image at the close
- Sentence openers +1: Patterned (-10) … Varied (+10). Half of sentences open with the subject.
  Pattern: Vary with a fronted time phrase
- Transitions -5: Implicit (-10) … Signposted (+10). Few explicit connectors.
  Pattern: Juxtapose instead of explaining
- Figurative density -4: Literal (-10) … Saturated (+10). About two figures per thousand words.
  Pattern: Brief similes from work and weather
- Dialogue tags -6: 'Said' only (-10) … Expressive beats (+10). Mostly 'said' or no tag.
  Pattern: Action beats instead of tags
- Dialogue rendering 0: Flat voices (-10) … Idiosyncratic speech (+10). Voices differ by word choice, not dialect.
  Pattern: Each speaker has one verbal habit
- Sonic ornamentation -7: None (-10) … Frequent soundplay (+10). No deliberate alliteration.
  Pattern: Rhythm comes from clause length
